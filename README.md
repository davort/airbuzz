# AirBuzz

AirBuzz makes a Google Fitbit Air vibrate for calls from people you choose and messages from apps you choose. Nothing else buzzes.

Works with Google Fitbit Air. AirBuzz is not made by, endorsed by or affiliated with Google or Fitbit. It talks to the Fitbit Air over an unofficial protocol, so a firmware update could stop it working.

## What it does

- **Calls**, from apps that post Android's incoming-call notification (confirmed with Google's Phone app, Truecaller and Signal): the Fitbit Air buzzes every second until you answer, decline or press Silence, for up to 60 s. People who ring is Anyone (the default for new installs), Contacts, or Chosen people. Quiet times and Pause silence calls too, except from people in Stay quiet's "Let some people ring anyway" list, which is off and empty until you fill it. Known issue: some phones' own phone apps, such as OnePlus Contacts on OxygenOS 16, post a ringing notification AirBuzz doesn't recognise yet, so their calls don't buzz; WhatsApp calls are untested.
- **Messages** from chosen apps: one, two or three taps. Messages from the same app within 15 s share one buzz.
- **Stay quiet** at night and in Do Not Disturb (both on by default), and while you use the phone (off by default).
- **Activity**: the last three alerts on Home and a week of history, each with what AirBuzz did and why.
- **Test buzz** shows how long the Fitbit Air took, step by step.
- **Appearance**: System, Light or Dark, with an accent colour for each.

## How it is put together

| Folder | What it does |
|---|---|
| `band/` | `AirBand` drives the Air over Bluetooth LE and Golden Gate: connect (or reuse the open link), wait for Google Health's traffic to go quiet, open a DTLS session with the band's public `BOOTSTRAP` key, write the haptic strength to make it buzz, then hand the channel back to Google Health. |
| `rules/` | Pure decisions: who may ring, which apps tap, quiet rules, the 15 s merge, phone number matching with libphonenumber. Unit tested. |
| `service/` | `AlertListener` (a notification listener) recognises incoming calls and messages. Android keeps a notification listener running, so there is no foreground service or permanent notification; the notifications are "Ringing your Fitbit Air" with Silence during a call, and one problem notification: silent while Bluetooth is off, loud once a call or message didn't reach the Fitbit Air (brief decision 41). |
| `data/` | Settings and the week's activity log, kept on the phone only. |
| `ui/` | Jetpack Compose screens in the approved look (design pass in Paper, brief decisions 20–39): rules as sentences with tinted blanks that each open one bottom sheet. `Theme.kt` holds the colour and type tokens; `Onboarding.kt` is the first-launch setup. |
| `Engine.kt` | Connects the two: one queue of band commands, where a call goes ahead of messages. |

Choices that came from measuring on a real Air (test app 0.2–0.4 in the private research repo):
- **The open link.** A listen-only Bluetooth client stays connected between buzzes; the secure session is still closed and handed back to Google Health after each one. That took a message buzz from a 3.1 s median to 0.5 s, and Google Health kept syncing over 41 minutes. A buzz that lands during a Google Health sync can take up to about 6 s.
- **The wrong key.** Every new Golden Gate stack is first offered a key AirBuzz doesn't have (`-10601`). Keeping the same stack up lets the band switch to `BOOTSTRAP`; restarting it fails every time.
- **The fast-link request** (`CONNECTION_PRIORITY_HIGH`) goes out at once on the open link, but on a new connection only after AirBuzz's own session exists, so it can't help Google Health win the handshake race.

Privacy: AirBuzz reads notifications to recognise calls and messages. It keeps the app, the caller or chat name, and what it did, for a week, on the phone. Its step log (shared only when you press Share log) names apps and outcomes, never message text or callers, and also keeps one week. It never stores message text and has no internet permission. The full policy is in [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md). The terms of use are in [TERMS.md](TERMS.md).

## Build

```
git submodule update --init third_party/golden-gate
git -C third_party/golden-gate submodule update --init external/lwip external/mbedtls
echo "sdk.dir=$ANDROID_HOME" > local.properties   # Android SDK with NDK 27.2.12479018 and CMake 3.22.1
./gradlew :app:testDebugUnitTest :app:assembleDebug
# APK: app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk
```

Releases: push a tag `v<versionName>`, such as `v0.7.0`. GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/release.yml`) runs the tests, builds an APK signed with the app signing key and a Play bundle signed with the same key (Play Console has it as both the app signing and the upload key), and puts the APK, its SHA-256 and the signing certificate's SHA-256 on a draft GitHub Release. The keys come from the `AIRBUZZ_KEYSTORE_B64` and `AIRBUZZ_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD` secrets; without them, `app/build.gradle.kts` builds exactly as before.
